Discovery call

You’re booked.

A confirmation with the time and the call link is on its way to your inbox. It’s twenty minutes with Dorian Leslie, and there’s nothing to prepare unless you’d like to.

If you have five minutes

Send these ahead.

None of it is required, but each one lets us spend the call on your project rather than on background. Reply to the confirmation email, or write to info@explorationsites.com.

  • Your current deck or website. A link is fine. It tells us where your story stands today.
  • The project and its stage. Name, location, and whether you are drilling, defining a resource, or heading toward a study.
  • What’s coming up. Results due, a raise, a conference, a rebrand. Dates help us scope honestly.
  • Any maps or data you already have. Whatever you have, in whatever state — rough is fine, and we’ll tell you on the call what matters for the work you have in mind. The most useful:
    Claims & tenure
    Claim or licence boundaries, with claim numbers.
    Drilling
    Collars (hole ID, easting, northing, elevation), downhole surveys (azimuth and dip), assays by interval, and lithology logs — or simply the drill-database export.
    Surface sampling
    Rock, soil, stream-sediment, or channel samples, with locations and results.
    Geology & geophysics
    Geological mapping, structures, and survey grids or interpretations (magnetics, IP, EM, gravity).
    Models
    Resource block models, wireframes, or a Leapfrog project, if sections or 3D are on the table.
    Context
    Roads, infrastructure, neighbouring deposits, topography or LiDAR, and imagery.
    Existing figures
    Past news-release maps, deck slides, or technical-report figures — PDFs are fine.
    Shapefile, GeoPackage, KML, CSV or Excel, DXF, and GeoTIFF all work, as do QGIS and ArcGIS projects. Tell us the coordinate system (for example, NAD83 UTM Zone 9N) if you know it.
