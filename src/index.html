Score your materials before you raise.

A generalist allocator makes the call in about ninety seconds — meeting, or pass. This is the desk they score you against.

Take the scorecard →
  • 01The one-line thesisA non-geologist can say what you have in one sentence — slide one, not slide nine.
  • 02Location in contextA locator map sets you beside the deposits and operators the market already knows.
  • 03The number that matters, shownGrade, width, and continuity on a section — not buried in a table a generalist skips.
  • 04A scale anchorTonnes, ounces, or contained metal framed against a known comparable — the size of the prize, at a glance.
  • 05A dated catalyst calendarWhat happens next — assays, a resource, a PEA — and roughly when. A reason to keep watching.
  • 06Money in, money outCash, burn, and what this raise actually funds — stated plainly, before they ask.
  • 07Capital structure at a glanceShares, options, warrants, and major holders — no dilution surprises three slides in.
  • 08A management record, not adjectivesPrior discoveries, builds, and exits stated factually — not the words “world-class.”
  • 09One visual languageMaps, sections, and slides that look like one company — not five vendors stapled together.
  • 10Continuity you can seeMineralisation in section or 3D, so the deposit holds together — not a scatter of plan-view dots.
  • 11It survives a phoneStill legible skimmed on a phone between meetings — type and figures that hold up small.
  • 12The forward testIt holds up when forwarded — without you in the room to narrate it. The one most decks fail.

Why We Exist

We are the

Markets are supposed to price discovery efficiently. In exploration, they don’t — the asset is buried, technical, and described in a language investors don’t speak. The result is a quiet, structural mispricing: brilliant geology that capital simply cannot see.

That gap is the problem we exist to close. We turn rigorous geology into a clear, fundable story — maps, decks, and sites built to survive an institutional desk — so the market can weigh what the deposit is actually worth.

Most IR spend buys decoration. We build the opposite: investor legibility — buried geology made legible to the people who price it.
