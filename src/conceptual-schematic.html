Investor Maps

Conceptual & schematic.

Conceptual and schematic figures — exploration models, deposit footprints, and stratigraphy — that frame the geological thesis for a non-technical audience.

Why it matters

The idea, before the data.

Most investors aren’t geologists. A conceptual figure does the work a wall of cross-sections can’t: it shows, in one clean diagram, what kind of deposit this is and why the company believes it’s here. Porphyry shell, epithermal vein system, orogenic gold at depth — the model is the thesis.

We build these as honest schematics, not artist’s impressions: a deposit model grounded in the real geology, a footprint comparison that sizes the opportunity against known analogues, or a stratigraphic column that explains the host. Accurate enough for the technical reader, clear enough for everyone else.

Selected Work

A few we’ve made.

Exploration model

The deposit model

A schematic of how the system is understood to work — host stratigraphy, the controls on mineralisation, and where the company expects grade to sit. The figure that lets a generalist follow the geological argument the rest of the deck is built on.

Exploration potential

The upside, framed

A multi-panel figure that walks from setting to target to potential — the story of why there is more to find. It frames blue-sky upside in a way that stays tied to the evidence rather than reaching past it.

Footprint comparison

Sized against the known

A project’s footprint set at true scale beside recognised analogues — the fastest way to convey magnitude without a single resource number. It answers the investor’s instinctive question: how big could this be?
