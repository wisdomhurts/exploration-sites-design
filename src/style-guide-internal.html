01  Who we are

The translation layer between deposit and capital markets.

If it carries our name, it will hold up in front of an institutional investor.

What we do

We are an investor-relations visualization studio in Victoria, BC. Since 1996 we have made investor maps, news-release drill maps and sections, 3D models, presentations, fact sheets, websites and brand identities for more than 300 exploration and mining companies on five continents.

Our job is to make a mineral project legible to a generalist investor without dumbing down the geology.

Who we make it for

Clients: CEOs, VPs of investor relations and geologists at junior and exploration-stage companies listed on the TSXV, CSE and ASX. They find us through referrals, conferences (PDAC, Roundup, AME) and search.

Their audience: portfolio managers, analysts and retail investors who have seen a thousand decks. Everything we make is judged by them, usually in seconds and often on a phone.

Five traits

AuthoritativeWe have seen hundreds of stories and know what works.
UnderstatedThe work speaks; we don’t shout.
MeticulousWe obsess over the detail the investor will never consciously notice.
CinematicWe are unafraid of scale and beauty when the geology earns it.
Sector-nativeWe are of this industry, not servicing it from outside. We know strike, dip and grade.
02  Voice and claims

Confident, precise, quietly expert.

The Economist meets a senior exploration geologist meets a fund’s quarterly letter.

How we write

  • Numbers over adjectives.“2.4 g/t Au over 48 m” beats “strong gold intercepts.”
  • Active voice. Short sentences for weight, longer ones for nuance.
  • Respect the reader. Assume they’ve read a thousand decks.
  • Say what we make and what it does for the reader. Leave out what it does for the share price.
  • Prices in Canadian dollars, always written “CAD”, before tax.

Words we reach for

Words we never use

In practice

NotOur world-class maps unlock your project’s true value.
WriteA section that shows where the intercept sits and how it connects to the last hole.
NotGuaranteed 48-hour turnaround.
WriteWithin two business days of clean data, for up to two maps per request.
NotAfter our rebrand, their market cap tripled.
WriteBrand, maps and website since 2021; about ten release maps a month.

Claims we don’t make

These come from our pre-launch claims review. They protect us and our clients’ disclosure obligations.

  • Share-price or market-cap outcomes, or any before-and-after valuation.We make materials investors can understand. We don’t move share prices.
  • “Guaranteed” anything. Turnaround is a standard we work to, with no stated remedy.
  • “Licensed geologists” or P.Geo. Say “geology- and GIS-trained mapmakers”.
  • Technical figures that haven’t been approved. The client’s Qualified Person reviews and approves every technical figure before release.
  • Client work that hasn’t been disclosed. Pre-release material stays confidential until the client publishes it.

Wordmark

Set in Inter Semibold, all lowercase, with 0.1 em letter-spacing, in Graphite (Quartz when reversed). The mark always sits to the left of the wordmark, vertically centred, with a gap of about a third of the mark’s width. Use the mark alone only where the name already appears nearby, such as favicons, social avatars and slide footers.

Clear space and size

Exploration Sites

Keep clear space on every side equal to half the mark’s height (the gold line above). Minimum size is 20 px tall for the mark on screen and 8 mm in print. On maps and slides, the lockup sits in a corner, never centred over content.

Do

  • Use the supplied files: logo-512.png and the SVG in the site’s navigation.
  • Put it on Quartz, Vellum, Graphite or a calm part of a photo.
  • Use the one-colour version when Ore can’t print accurately.

Don’t

  • Recolour the ticks or the cross, or swap their colours.
  • Stretch, rotate, outline or add a shadow or glow.
  • Use the old pastel or multicolour versions of the logo.
  • Set the wordmark in the serif or in capitals.

05  Typography

Two voices. Never a third.

Source Serif 4 for headlines and display numbers. Inter for everything else. Both are free from Google Fonts.

Rules

  • Tabular figures in every table and data label.
  • Curly quotes and apostrophes, true ellipses (…), real en dashes for ranges (3–7).
  • Uppercase only for short labels, with letter-spacing.
  • Headlines in sentence case.

Avoid

  • A third typeface, including monospace. The mono data voice was retired in July 2026.
  • Bold and italic together.
  • All-caps paragraphs or headlines.
  • Calibri, Arial or Helvetica as stand-ins. Install the real fonts.
06  Layout and visual style

Restraint is the luxury.

Structure comes from whitespace, type hierarchy and 1 px rules.

Principles

  • The work is the proof.Show the map, the deck, the site. Don’t describe it when you can show it.
  • Evidence before assertions.Every claim has a source and survives a skeptical portfolio manager.
  • Subtract before adding.Whitespace is the most expensive element on the page.
  • Editorial rhythm.A small label, a serif headline, then content: two columns, separated by thin rules.

Never

GlowGradient textDecorative orbsHeavy drop shadowsBouncy animationNeon on darkGlassmorphismStock-photo handshakesEmoji as icons

07  Maps and figures

Every figure is read in seconds, often on a phone.

These rules apply to everything we draw. The colours are the client’s; the discipline is ours.

  1. One message per map.Decide the single thing it shows: a new intercept, a step-out, continuity along strike. Remove everything that doesn’t support it.
  2. Plan first, then section.A plan shows where you drilled; a section shows what you hit. Pair them for headline intercepts.
  3. Show grade honestly.Use true scale and the same grade bins and colours every release. Label core length or true width exactly as the QP specifies.
  4. Give a scale anchor.Add a scale bar, a north arrow and one reference a non-geologist recognises: a road, the property boundary, a known deposit.
  5. Keep the base locked.Use the same base, symbols and layout every release, so a run of releases reads as one advancing story.
  6. Make it survive a phone.Check it at phone width. If hole labels or the legend aren’t legible, there’s too much on the map.
  7. Make it hold up when forwarded.Put the title, project name, date and key intercept on the figure itself.
  8. Get QP sign-off.The client’s Qualified Person approves every technical figure before it’s released.

Turnaround on the News-Release Map Program is two business days from clean, complete data (collars, surveys, assays and the text for the map), for up to two maps per request. Never promise faster in writing.

09  Before it goes out

The last pass.

Run through this for anything that carries our name.

  • Would this hold up in front of a portfolio manager?
  • Is there one clear message?
  • Numbers instead of adjectives; no banned words.
  • No share-price outcomes and no “guaranteed”.
  • Technical figures approved by the client’s QP.
  • No undisclosed client information.
  • Two typefaces only; tabular figures in data.
  • Quartz, not white; Ore at 2% or less.
  • No glow, gradients or heavy shadows.
  • Checked at phone width.
  • Name, URL and phone written exactly as in section 08.
  • Prices in CAD, marked “from”, before tax.
