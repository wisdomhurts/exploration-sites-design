The Fundable Materials Scorecard.

Twelve things a generalist allocator looks for in your deck, maps, and website in the first ninety seconds — before they decide whether your geology is worth a meeting. Score your own materials against the desk: one point each.

  1. 01

    The one-line thesis

    A non-geologist can say what you have and why it matters in a single sentence — on slide one, not slide nine.

  2. 02

    Location in context

    A locator map places the project beside the deposits and operators the market already knows. The comps do the explaining.

  3. 03

    The number that matters, shown

    Grade, width, and continuity are visualised — on a section or long-section — not buried in a table a generalist will skip.

  4. 04

    A scale anchor

    Tonnes, ounces, or contained metal are framed against a known comparable, so the size of the prize is legible at a glance.

  5. 05

    A dated catalyst calendar

    What happens next — assays, a maiden resource, a PEA — and roughly when. A reason to keep watching after they close the deck.

  6. 06

    Money in, money out

    Cash position, burn rate, and what the current raise actually funds — stated plainly, before they have to ask.

  7. 07

    Capital structure at a glance

    Shares outstanding, options and warrants, and major holders — no dilution surprises waiting three slides in.

  8. 08

    A management record, not adjectives

    Prior discoveries, builds, or exits stated factually. Credibility is shown with track record, not the word “world-class.”

  9. 09

    One visual language

    Maps, sections, and slides look like one company — consistent palette, type, and symbology — not five vendors stapled together.

  10. 10

    Continuity you can see

    Mineralisation shown in section or 3D so an investor believes the deposit holds together — not just a scatter of plan-view dots.

  11. 11

    It survives a phone

    The deck still reads when it’s skimmed on a phone between meetings — legible type, figures that hold up small.

  12. 12

    The forward test

    The story holds up when the deck is forwarded to a colleague — without you in the room to narrate it. This is the one most materials fail.

Score it honestly. 9–12 — your materials are doing their job. 5–8 — the geology is real but the market can’t fully read it. Under 5 — the market probably can’t see what you actually have, for reasons that have nothing to do with the rocks.

This scorecard measures how clearly your materials communicate the geology — not your valuation. What the market does with a clear story is its own decision.
