Why it matters

Where grade lives.

A plan map shows where you drilled. A section shows what you hit. For an investor weighing a discovery, the question that decides the valuation is the one a section answers: does the mineralisation continue at depth, and does it hold its grade?

We build cross-sections and long sections straight from your collar, survey, and assay tables — intercepts logged against lithology and structure, grade shown honestly, and a vertical scale that doesn’t flatter the body. Long sections project the whole deposit onto one plane, so an investor can see continuity, plunge, and open extensions in a single figure.

Selected Work

A few we’ve made.

Long section

Continuity, on one plane

Every hole on a vein or zone projected onto a single section — intercepts colour-coded by grade, the mineralised envelope drawn, and the open extensions left obvious. The figure that shows an investor the deposit is real, continuous, and not yet closed off.

Cross-section

Grade against geology

A vertical slice through the drill fence — hole traces, logged lithology, and significant intervals shown in true relation. It ties the headline intercept from a news release back to its geological context, so the number is read as evidence, not just a figure.

Composite

Plan & section, paired

A plan and its section set side by side, keyed to the same holes and the same colour scale. The pairing lets an investor move between map view and depth view without losing the thread — the format that anchors a resource update or a discovery deck.
