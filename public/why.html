Why We Exist

Capital can’t fund what it can’t read.

Markets are supposed to price discovery efficiently. In exploration, they don’t — because the asset is buried, technical, and described in a language investors don’t speak. The result is a quiet, structural mispricing: brilliant geology that capital simply cannot see. That gap is the problem we exist to close.

The Investor’s Problem

An information asymmetry, not a geology problem.

A generalist fund manager reviews dozens of opportunities a week. They are not a geologist. They cannot personally verify a cross-section, reconcile a resource model, or weigh one greenstone belt against another. So they do what every rational allocator does under uncertainty: they default to no.

The deposit may be extraordinary. But if the story arrives illegible — a dense deck, a confusing map, a website that signals 2008 — the investor never accumulates enough conviction to act. The rocks aren’t rejected. They’re never understood in the first place.

Economists call it information asymmetry: the seller knows what the asset is worth, the buyer can’t verify it, and value goes unfunded. In exploration, it’s the single most expensive problem on the cap table.

The Window

You get about two minutes before the verdict.

Decision-makers spend a fraction of a meeting deciding whether the rest of it is worth their attention. Years of fieldwork, drilling, and geological insight get compressed into a first impression measured in seconds.

Years in the groundSeconds on the page

The whole investment thesis is judged inside that narrow gold band. Clarity isn’t decoration — it’s the only thing that survives the scan.

The Asymmetry, Itemised

The deposit is fixed. The comprehension is variable.

Nothing underground changes between a failed raise and a funded one. What changes is whether the market can hold the story in its head long enough to write a cheque. The left column is constant. The right column is what we engineer.

Our Position

We are the translation layer between a deposit and the capital markets.

We started in 1996 as mapmakers — take the data, make it clean, get it into the news / report / deck. Three decades and 500+ projects later, the discipline is the same and the mandate is larger: convert invisible, buried, technical data into something an allocator can understand in the time they actually have.

How The Translation Works

Three moves close the gap.

01

Locate

We put the project on the map an investor already trusts — flanked by the deposits and districts the market knows — so scale and context register in seconds, not slides.

02

Clarify

Geological maps, cross sections, and 3D models that make grade, continuity, and upside legible — the technical case rendered so a generalist can read it like a specialist.

03

Carry

One visual language across the deck, the website, the films, and the brand — so every touchpoint compounds conviction instead of resetting it. The whole investor system, coherent.

The Belief

Capital flows toward clarity.

It always has. The companies that present their science with precision and beauty are the ones that get funded — not because the market is shallow, but because clarity is the only thing an allocator can actually evaluate under time pressure.

We don’t move the rocks and we don’t take credit for the drill results. We make the case legible — so that when the geology is ready to be seen, the capital is ready to see it. If it carries our name, it holds up in front of an institutional investor. That is the entire bar, and the entire business.

You have the geology. Make it investable.

If the market isn’t pricing what’s in the ground, the problem is legibility — and legibility is fixable.
