## A. News-Release Map Program page — FAQ  [Q1–Q4]

**How fast can I get a drill-results map for a news release?**
On the News-Release Map Program, within two business days of clean, complete data
(collar, survey and assay tables plus the text you want on the map), for up to two maps
per request. Larger batches get a delivery schedule agreed with you. One-off maps outside
the program typically take 3–7 business days.

**How much does a news-release map cost?**
A one-off map starts at $1,200 CAD. On the Map Program, maps come as monthly capacity:
Cadence is $2,500/month for up to 4 maps, Active Drill $3,500 for up to 8, and Full
Program $4,500 for up to 12–15. There's a one-time $2,500 setup and a four-month minimum.
Extra maps are $750 each. All prices are in Canadian dollars, before tax.

**What do you need from us?**
Claim boundaries and your drilling to date: collars, downhole surveys and assays, or
simply the drill-database export. For each new set of results, send the new holes' data.
Your QP reviews and approves every map before release.

**Can we pause between drill programs?**
Yes. You can pause for up to six months, and your locked base map is kept. Paused months
don't count toward the four-month minimum.

## B. Investor presentations page — FAQ  [Q11–Q13]

**How much does an investor presentation for a mining company cost?**
Decks start at $5,000 CAD and typically take 4–6 weeks, depending on the number of maps
and figures, and how much of the story is being rebuilt.

**What should a junior mining company's investor presentation include?**
A one-line thesis on slide one; the project in its regional context; the key result shown
on a section, not buried in a table; a scale anchor against known deposits; a dated
catalyst calendar; cash, burn and what the raise funds; capital structure; and a factual
management record. Our free
[Fundable Materials Scorecard](/fundable-materials-scorecard.html) lists all twelve
criteria a generalist investor checks.

**Do you make the maps in the deck too?**
Yes. Locator, property, geoscience, drill and 3D figures are drawn to one standard, so
the deck, fact sheet and website look like one company.

## C. Websites page — FAQ  [Q16–Q18]

**What should a mining company's investor website include?**
At minimum: Projects, News, Investors, Corporate and Contact sections, plus a live share
quote, a news-release archive and links to your SEDAR+ filings. The project pages should
answer an analyst's next questions (location, geology, sections, resources, catalysts)
without a phone call.

**Webflow or WordPress for a junior mining company?**
Webflow suits companies that want a polished, design-led site that's quick to launch and
easy to keep current, with less plugin maintenance. WordPress suits companies that need
bespoke functionality, such as multi-language sites, complex document libraries or deep
integrations, or that already run on it. We build and maintain both.

**How much does an investor website cost, and how long does it take?**
WordPress sites start at $12,000 CAD and Webflow sites at $18,000. Both typically take
about five weeks.

## Guide 1 — "How to make a drill-results map investors understand"  [Q9, Q3, Q5]

*~700 words. Proposed URL: `/guides/drill-results-maps.html`.*

1. **One message per map.** Decide the single thing the map must show, such as a new
   intercept, a step-out or continuity along strike, and remove everything that doesn't
   support it.
2. **Plan first, then section.** A plan shows where you drilled; a section shows what you
   hit. Pair them for headline intercepts.
3. **Show grade honestly.** Use true scale, consistent grade bins across releases, and
   intervals labelled as core length or true width as your QP specifies.
4. **Give a scale anchor.** Add a scale bar, a north arrow, and one known reference such
   as a nearby deposit, the property boundary or a road, so a non-geologist can size it.
5. **Keep the base locked.** The same base, colours and symbols every release let
   investors compare results across months without re-learning the map.
6. **Make it survive a phone.** Most people see release figures on a phone, so labels and
   intercept callouts must stay legible small.
7. **Make it hold up when forwarded.** It should still make sense with nobody there to
   explain it.
8. **What to have ready.** Collar, survey and assay tables (or the database export), claim
   boundaries, the intercepts to call out, and the coordinate system.

Closes with a short note on the News-Release Map Program and one internal link.

## Guide 2 — "What a junior mining company's investor website needs"  [Q17, Q18, Q16]

*~600 words. Proposed URL: `/guides/mining-investor-website.html`.*

- **The five sections every analyst expects:** Projects, News, Investors, Corporate,
  Contact.
- **The IR plumbing:** a live quote, the news-release archive, SEDAR+ links, the current
  deck and fact sheet, and capital structure.
- **Project pages that answer the next question:** location map, geology, key sections,
  resources (with their category and QP), catalysts.
- **Keeping it current:** stale figures cost credibility; decide who updates what, and
  how fast after a release.
- **Webflow vs WordPress:** the same comparison as FAQ C.
- **Hosting and security:** CDN, SSL, backups, monitoring.
